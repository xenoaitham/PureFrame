# Legal Disclaimer & Scope

PureFrame is an open-source tool designed to allow individuals to automatically sanitize objectionable content from media files they **already legally possess** for private, in-home viewing.

## The Family Movie Act of 2005

In the United States, the [Family Movie Act of 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Movie_Act_of_2005) (Title II of the Family Entertainment and Copyright Act) limits copyright infringement liability when making imperceptible portions of a motion picture audio or video content during a performance in a private household. A key condition of this safe harbor is that "no fixed copy of the altered version" is created. 

Since PureFrame currently generates an output video file, the direct applicability of the Family Movie Act is uncertain and untested. PureFrame is intended for **US-focused, private use only**. It does not bypass DRM and should only be used on files you legally own. **This is not legal advice.**

While PureFrame does generate an output file, its core intended use is completely aligned with the spirit of allowing families to filter content they have already purchased.

## Strict Operational Boundaries

PureFrame operates within the following strict boundaries to ensure legal compliance:

1. **No Media Distribution:** PureFrame does not download, share, distribute, host, or upload any media files.
2. **Local Execution Only:** The application and its AI models run entirely on your local hardware. No video frames or audio samples are ever sent to a cloud server.
3. **No Streaming Subversion:** PureFrame refuses to operate on streaming URLs. It only processes local files.
4. **Respect for DRM:** PureFrame does not bypass Digital Rights Management (DRM) or encryption. It will simply fail to read DRM-protected files.
5. **No Built-in Content:** The software comes with no copyrighted media.

By using PureFrame, you acknowledge that you are responsible for the source files you provide to it and that you are using it in compliance with your local copyright laws for private viewing.
